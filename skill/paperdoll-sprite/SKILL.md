# Paperdoll sprite skill

Produces a side-view SVG (64x64 by default, bigger when the animal or its props need it) where every body part is its own named layer, joints are marked with paired pivot markers, and all art is neutral grey so the engine can tint it.

**Worked examples** (all approved; read the one closest to your animal before drawing anything new). Each has `references/<name>.svg`, `<name>.hierarchy.json`, and its generator `build_<name>.py` (the builds are **relocatable**: they find their inputs in `references/sources/` and write `<name>_raw.svg` + `<name>.hierarchy.json` to the current directory, so `python3 -I references/build_wolf.py` works from any empty folder; then run `normalize.py` and `validate.py`. Verified: all four rebuild byte-identical to the approved dolls):
- `deer` (64x77): the user's hand-authored deer with the Epochs stag head, ear and antlers; canvas grown for the antlers.
- `boar` (79x66): the Epochs boar head on a hand-built body; spline/spike/warp helpers.
- `wolf` (64x54): fully traced from line art; the best template for mask-based builds (shoulder/hip pivots, joint tone discs).
- `bull` (74x54): traced like the wolf, plus horn layers, and leg tone forced to the body's tone at each pivot. The tail is DRAWN (two capsules with a round root cap on the pivot) with only the black tuft traced; a traced thin tail merged with the rump's dark edge and left a sliver when it swung. The body polygon includes the whole haunch so a swinging hind leg leaves a rounded rump.
- `rabbit` (50x50): traced like the wolf from a walking pose with a raised foreleg: the standing near foreleg and near hind leg are reused for both sides (dead side-on), long ears are two layers, ear/head tones smoothed with a larger `open_px` and the far ear kept at the reference tones (a darker far ear showed a seam against the head).
- `man` (40x64): the traced version of the humanoid template. Head (hair, beard, face), hand and foot are traced from the sample; the torso is drawn (12 points); arms and legs are rebuilt as `capsule` ball joints hanging straight down, because the sample is a stride pose and cannot be a dead-side-on rest pose. The sample's fig leaf was removed at the user's request; garments that hang over the legs belong in their own layer drawn after the near leg (see 'Tracing a character'). Neck: keep the shoulder cap below the torso top (shoulder pivot y about 20) so the neck column stays visible.
- `humanoid` (40x64): procedural biped built from capsules; the template for anything that is not a quadruped.

## Output spec (hard rules; `scripts/validate.py` enforces them)

- Canvas defaults to 64x64 (`viewBox="0 0 W H"`, origin at 0,0), animal faces **left**, ground line near y=57-62, shadow ellipse under it. **Do not squeeze a big animal into 64x64**: keep the deer's scale (about 1 unit per px of a 64px sprite) and widen the canvas instead (the boar is 76x66). Nothing may sit at negative coordinates or outside the canvas.
- One Inkscape layer per part (`inkscape:groupmode="layer"`, unique `inkscape:label`). Z-order = layer order, back to front. Layer order for quadrupeds: `shadow, tail, leg-br-u, leg-br-l, leg-fr-u, leg-fr-l, body, neck, leg-bl-u, leg-bl-l, leg-fl-u, leg-fl-l, ear-r, [horn-r], head, ear-l, [horn-l | antlers]` (far horn behind the head, near horn/antlers in front). `b/f` = back/front, `l/r` = left/right, `-u/-l` = upper/lower segment. `r` legs and ear are the far side.
- **Limbs are ball-jointed.** Split each limb at the joint, then close each half with a **round cap centred exactly on the pivot** (a semi-ellipse about half the limb width deep, kept inside the limb outline). The upper segment ends in a round bulge just below the pivot, the lower starts with a round top just above it, and nothing else of the upper runs past the joint. When either half rotates its cap turns inside the other, so the silhouette never opens or notches. This is how `references/deer.svg` does it at the hind knee. A flat cut or a whole-limb 'upper' segment is a bug (the second shows as a ghost leg when the lower swings); `validate.py` flags both.
- **Dead side-on, always.** No 3/4 stance, no far-side offset. Left and right legs are **exact copies**: same shapes, same pivots (the far pair is drawn behind and may be one tone step darker). Extract or draw ONE foreleg and ONE hind leg and reuse them. `validate.py` fails any doll whose left/right legs differ.
- **Legs pivot from the shoulder and hip, never the elbow.** The upper segment is the whole limb from the shoulder/hip joint down to the wrist/hock: its top end sits inside the body with a round cap centred on that pivot (`Ref.top_cap`), its lower end gets the knee/hock cap. The lower segment is wrist/hock to paw.
- Pivots: small red unfilled ellipse (r 0.34, stroke `#ff0000` 0.1). Every joint gets a **pair at the identical point**: `<child>-pivot` inside the parent layer, `<parent>-pivot` inside the child layer. Hierarchy: body > neck > head > ears/extras; body > tail; body > leg-*-u > leg-*-l. Do not hand-place pivots: write a hierarchy JSON and run `normalize.py`.
- Colour: fills are neutral greys only (R=G=B). Tone tokens and values: `eye #0b0b0b`, `hoof #242424`, `nose #484848`, `far #636363` (far-side parts), `dark #757575` (main mass), `mid #b5b5b5`, `bone #dbdbdb`, `light #dcdcdc` (belly/muzzle/inner ear/highlight). Shadow: `#000` at 0.4 opacity. The engine tints by luminance, so keep value steps clear of each other. No gradients, filters, images, or blurs.
- Shapes are flat, hard-edged fills, no outlines (strokes only where a thin feature like antler tines needs them). Label shapes `<layer>-shade-<tone>` (e.g. `neck-shade-light`); `normalize.py` renames them.
- Hand-built parts: one `dark` base shape + a few `light` shapes laid over it. Traced parts: a gap-free tone tiling instead (see the extraction section). Either way detail lives in silhouette and tone, not in extra lines.

## Geometry rules (most important)

- **Fidelity over simplicity.** A part is 10-24 points, not 3-6. Place a point at every real inflection of the silhouette (brow, bridge, snout tip, jaw, heel, hock, bulge of a haunch), not at even spacing. Use `pathutil.spline` (Catmull-Rom through your points) and list the true corners (snout tip, hoof corners, ear tip, mane bristles) as sharp indices. Raw hand-typed beziers with 3 nodes will look like a blob.
- **Measure, don't eyeball.** Read coordinates off a unit grid placed over the reference (`grid.py`), then check every part with `overlay.py --outline` against the reference before colouring. Compare nose-tip, brow, hoof positions and body edges; fix any point more than ~0.5 unit off.
- **Calibrate first.** If the user's file embeds the reference as `<image x y width height>`, scale = width/pixels and x,y is the top-left; otherwise match two far-apart landmarks (e.g. near-front hoof and near-hind hoof) to get scale and offset.
- **Cap the joints.** Vector outlines: `pathutil.cap_split(limb_outline, cut_y)` returns (upper, lower, pivot) with exact curve subdivision and round caps. Mask-based builds: `Ref.limb_cap(mask, cut_y)` (disc centred on the joint, clipped to the silhouette). Put the pivot at the returned centre, then pose the limb with `pose.py` or the viewer at 45-60 degrees and look at the knee.
- **Change proportions with a warp, not by redrawing.** To lengthen a snout, leg or neck, apply a smooth x/y warp over the affected range to every shape in it (`wp()` in `references/build_boar.py`): full shift where detail must stay rigid (nostril, hoof), zero where it must not move, smoothstep between. Keep eyes, tusks and other attachments either rigid or in the same warp, and draw any part that should look 'behind' the head first so the head silhouette clips it.
- **Eyes get their own measured pass**: zoom to ~100x on the eye, read lid corners, tilt, iris/sclera split, catch-lights and the shadowed socket, and build socket (`far`), ball (`eye`), sclera sliver (`mid`), catch-light (`light`) as separate shapes.
- **Head, neck and body get the same ball joints.** Pick the pivot at the middle of the junction, give BOTH parts the disc around it (`cap = disc_mask & fg`, OR-ed into each part's mask) and force that disc to one tone. In the deer the neck runs past the head pivot with a round top and the head extends back past it with a round back; a rounded rear edge on the head (as on the boar) does the same job. Rotate the head +/-30 degrees and the neck +/-10 and check there is no notch at the pivot.
- **Fur: two tones, joints blended.** Reduce the fur to two tones (`levels=[one threshold]`; a separate horn/hoof tone is fine as a third level). Put a forced-tone disc on every joint (`Ref.force_tone_disc`: shoulder, hip, knee/hock, neck base, head base, tail base) so both sides of every cap are the same tone, and flatten limb interiors that sit inside the torso (`tone_partition(force=...)`) to **the tone the body has at that pivot** (`force_tone_disc` returns it; the wolf is dark there, the bull is light, so a fixed 'dark' leaves dark blocks on a light body), otherwise shading patterns fail to line up when a limb rotates and the cap edges show as hard cut-offs. Keep disc radii small (about the limb half-width): big ones leave round blobs.
- Depth shows in tone: lit mass `dark`, shaded areas (belly, far side, ruff, mane) `far`, small highlights `mid`/`light`. Not in outlines.

## Extracting geometry from clean line-art references

When the reference is flat line art on a plain background (e.g. the Epochs samples, any vector-style or cel-shaded animal), do **not** read points by eye: use `scripts/trace.py` (see `references/build_wolf.py` for a full worked build).

1. Mirror the reference so the animal faces left; pick a scale (about 0.03-0.04 sprite units per pixel keeps animals at deer scale) and an offset; check placement with `grid.py`.
2. `Ref` extracts the exact silhouette (outer edge of the outline) and the fill tone levels. Define each part as a clipping polygon in sprite units: head, ears, neck, body, tail, and one polygon per leg. Cut where the joint will be; polygons may overlap, and `part_mask(..., grow=0.35)` overlaps the cut edges so parts never leave seams.
3. Each part is a **gap-free tone tiling** (`tone_partition`): every fill level is traced as its own shape (levels map to `far`/`dark`/`mid`). Do not draw a base shape plus overlays: that shows rectangular seams where a part's cut edge crosses a different tone.
4. Limbs: take the leg polygon from the shoulder/hip down, `binary_opening` it (about 13 px) to drop thin fur spikes, `Ref.top_cap` it on the shoulder/hip pivot, then `Ref.limb_cap(mask, cut)` for the knee/hock (it fits the cap inside the limb and returns the pivot x). Make ONE foreleg and ONE hind leg and emit them twice (far copy through a one-step-darker tone map).
5. Line work worth keeping (eye, mouth, nostril dots, toe lines) is added by hand or with `interior_lines` as `far`/`eye` shapes. Dark features found automatically (`dark_blobs`) must be limited to small zones around the eye and nostril: the thick outline stroke around a head otherwise matches too and shows as a black outline (happened on the bull). Thin fur streaks are dropped on purpose; the style is line-less.
6. Tune `tol` (0.12-0.18 keeps fur tufts) and check with `overlay.py --outline` and the viewer.

### Dark fills, strokes and big props (learned on the Epochs boar and stag)

- Brown line art often has dark fills as dark as the outline. `Ref(..., line_lum=52, thick_px=14)` treats thick dark regions as fill (ear interior, jaw) and thin strokes as line work; set `thick_px` above half the outline thickness. Use 3-4 `levels` and map classes to tones per part (e.g. head `{0:'dark',1:'mid'}`).
- Thin interior strokes (tine outlines inside antlers) form closed loops. Trace them with ONE even-odd path (`fill-rule:evenodd`, all contours together) or each loop fills as a solid disc.
- Cut-box edges: when two parts are cut from one drawing, make sure the boxes overlap; a gap between the boxes shows as a background slit when the head rotates (a hairline slot beside the boar's ear). Check by posing, not at rest.
- **Props with open gaps (antlers, branches, spears):** the usual silhouette fills enclosed holes, which fills the gaps between crossing tines. Use `part_mask(..., holes=True)` (`fg_raw`), find the fill pieces (connected components of the cream fill), and trace one shape per piece, dilated by half the outline stroke so the strokes are absorbed. Tone the pieces by depth (front lighter, behind darker) instead of drawing separator lines; thin dashed lines looked broken.
- **Cut polygons must contain only their own part.** A leg polygon that reaches into the tail drags a strip of tail fur into the leg layer; it swings with the hip and looks like a detached tuft. Pose every limb and look for slivers. Remove thin fur spikes that hang past a joint with `binary_opening` (about 0.45 units) on the limb mask, and raise the minimum size for isolated tone flecks in body and legs.
- `Ref.limb_cap(..., fit=True)` shrinks the joint disc until it sits fully inside the limb; a disc wider than the limb is clipped into square corners that stick out when bent. Flat tone boxes inside a limb must run down to the joint's tone zone, not stop on a hard line (it shows as a rectangle on the far leg).
- **Black fur is outline-dark.** A black tail tuft or dark mane has the same luminance as the outline, so the extractor drops it to the neighbouring (wrong, light) tone and it shows as a separate patch with a gap. Force that area to a fur tone with a polygon (`tone_partition(force=[(poly_mask, k)])`).
- Body/neck cuts need generous overlap (`part_mask(..., grow=1.0)`) and a big joint cap (radius about 6) at a heavy neck, or a slit opens at the shoulder when the neck turns.
- After ANY change re-sync the doll into `references/` and rerun `make_viewer.py`; the viewer embeds a copy and will otherwise keep showing the old version.
- A prop bigger than the canvas (antlers) means growing the canvas, not shrinking the prop: shift the whole doll down by the overshoot, update width/height/viewBox and all pivots (`references/build_deer.py`).
- Parts keep their silhouette from the sample, but joint pivots, hierarchy names and the transplanted details (e.g. the boar's nostril pad) are decisions to make explicitly.

## Non-quadrupeds (humanoids, birds, creatures with arms)

Same spec, different part names. Use `references/build_humanoid.py` as the template.

- **Names:** root `body` (torso + pelvis), `neck`, `head`, and limbs as `leg-l-u`, `leg-l-l`, `leg-r-u`, `leg-r-l`, `arm-l-u`, `arm-l-l`, `arm-r-u`, `arm-r-l` (`l` near, `r` far; `-u` hip/shoulder to knee/elbow, `-l` knee/elbow to foot/hand). Feet and hands are shapes inside the lower segment, not separate layers, unless they must articulate (then `foot-l`, `hand-l`, children of the lower segment, with their own pivots). Extras get layers of their own (`tail`, `wing-l-u`, `cape`, `hat`) and join the hierarchy from the part they hang on.
- **Hierarchy:** body > neck > head; body > arm-*-u > arm-*-l; body > leg-*-u > leg-*-l. Pivots: hip, knee, shoulder, elbow, neck base, head base, always as pairs at identical points (`normalize.py` makes them from the hierarchy JSON).
- **Ball joints by construction:** `pathutil.capsule(p0, p1, r0, r1)` returns a tapered segment with round caps centred on p0 and p1. Build the upper segment as `capsule(hip, knee, r_hip, r_knee)` and the lower as `capsule(knee, ankle, r_knee, r_ankle)` with the SAME centre and radius at the knee, and the joint is a ball joint with no further work. Add feet, hands, cuffs as blobs in the same layer.
- **Dead side-on still applies:** near and far limbs are identical copies at the same position, the far side one tone step darker; the validator checks `leg-l`/`leg-r` and `arm-l`/`arm-r` like the quadruped pairs, and the same cap checks run on `leg-*-u`/`arm-*-u`.
- **Tracing a character from line art** works like the animal builds: clip head, torso, and one arm and one leg from the silhouette with polygons, cap them with `top_cap`/`limb_cap`, and force the tone at every joint. Arms that hang over the torso need the tone forced to the sleeve at the shoulder, or the cap shows as a blob.
- **Tracing a character (learned on `man`):** (1) A stride or action pose cannot be the rest pose: trace what is pose-independent (head, torso, hands, feet, cloth) and rebuild limbs as capsules at the measured joints. (2) Dark textured regions (hair, beard) are full of dark strokes, so the usual tone classifier fails (hair came out as skin); build `Ref(..., class_sigma=7)` to classify by the local mean of non-stroke pixels. (3) Without outlines the near arm vanishes against the torso when both are the same skin tone: give the torso one tone step darker than limbs and head. (4) Clothes that hang over the legs (loincloth, skirt, coat tails) are their own layer drawn after the near leg, found here by colour (`G - R > 8`). (5) Dark-feature zones for eyes must start inside the face edge or they catch the silhouette outline. (6) Nose, mouth and brows are thin strokes the tracer drops: draw them with `capsule` segments. (7) Hands/feet: trace each as ONE silhouette (`R.paths` on the part mask), not a tone tiling, which shreds small shapes into slivers; hands in the same tone as the legs vanish when they hang over them, so give the legs the torso's darker step. A head cut-box that reaches below the beard drags a collar of shoulder skin into the head layer. (8) A three-quarter drawing is far wider than a side-on torso; cut the torso polygon to a side-on width (about 9 units for a 55-unit figure) or it drags the sample's own arm and shoulder masses in and, with a capsule arm in the middle, shows as detached grey slabs either side of the arm.
- **Viewer:** the doll viewer recognises `leg-l-u` style names and animates idle, walk, trot (jog) and run with the arms swinging against the legs; birds, wings and tails need their own `set(...)` lines in `animPose` in `scripts/viewer_template.html`.
- **Limits of the example:** its proportions and pelvis are rough (the hip caps are wider than the pelvis band); redraw the torso/pelvis splines for a real character, keep the joints.

## Workflow

0. If the user supplies a half-finished file, keep its layer names, ordering and pivots intent; list its parts with `pathutil.py info`, render it, overlay it on the reference, and say honestly which shapes are loose before replacing them. Never overwrite the user's file; write a new one.
1. **Study the reference.** View it. Note: silhouette proportions (body length : height : leg length), head shape and angle, distinguishing features (tusks, mane ridge, ears, tail), and where the light value areas are (belly, muzzle, chest). If the reference is not a clean side view, say which features you are inferring.
2. **Block out in a scratch SVG** with one flat silhouette per part, in the right z-order. Render with `scripts/render.py sprite.svg out.png --scale 12 --ref reference.png` and look at it next to the reference. Fix proportions first; detail later.
3. **Shape the parts.** Hand-built: 10-24 spline points per part (see Geometry rules). Traced: use the extraction section. Compare against the closest worked example.
4. **Add light/dark shapes and features** (eye, nose, hoof, tusk, mane).
5. **Rig.** Write `<name>.hierarchy.json` (copy `references/deer.hierarchy.json`, new coordinates at the visual joints: shoulder, hip, knee/hock, neck base, head base, ear base), then
   `python3 -I scripts/normalize.py in.svg hierarchy.json out.svg`.
6. **Verify.** `python3 -I scripts/validate.py out.svg [--size WxH]` must pass. Render with and without pivots; `--solo <layer>` for each moving part; then pose it with `scripts/pose.py` (rotate legs, head, tail) and check there are no gaps or limbs detaching.
7. Iterate steps 2-6 at least twice before reporting. Always show the user the final render next to the reference.

## Pitfalls

- Do not copy label mistakes from other files in a project (drafts and early attempts); the four `references/*.svg` are the trusted examples.
- Far-side limbs are exact copies of the near limbs at the same position (dead side-on), drawn behind in a one-step-darker tone. Never offset them; they read when the cycle moves them out of phase.
- Keep the pivot inside or at the edge of both parts; a pivot far outside the shape makes rotations swing wildly.
- Stay inside the canvas including antlers, ears, tail and shadow; leave a 2px margin.
- Don't trace photo textures. The target is a stylised, simplified silhouette, not the reference's linework.

## Tools

- `scripts/make_viewer.py out.html [sprite.svg ...]`: builds the **doll viewer**, one self-contained HTML page. Open it in a browser and drop any rigged sprite on it (hierarchy is read from the paired pivots; no JSON). Idle / walk / trot / run cycles, a manual pose mode with a slider per joint, mirror, tint test, pivot display and a scrolling ground. URL hash for scripted screenshots: `#sample=wolf&anim=run&t=0.3&paused=1`. Always run a new doll through it before calling it done. Biped names are animated too (see the non-quadruped section).
- `scripts/trace.py`: silhouette, tone-level and dark-blob extraction from line-art references; Bezier/spline outlines via RDP. See the section above.
- `scripts/grid.py`: reference crop with a labelled grid in sprite units, to read coordinates (`--pts` plots candidate nodes).
- `scripts/overlay.py`: sprite over reference at known placement; `--outline` draws edges only. The main geometry check.
- `scripts/pathutil.py`: parse/transform paths, `spline`, `split_y`, `cap_split`, `capsule` (round-capped limb segment), node counts (`info FILE`).
- `scripts/pose.py`: rotate layers about their pivots with children following; proves the rig. `--solo layerA,layerB` isolates layers, which is how stray slivers and tail/leg mix-ups are found.
- `scripts/render.py`: SVG to PNG (rsvg-convert), `--ref` side-by-side, `--solo/--hide` layers, `--no-pivots`.
- `scripts/validate.py`, `scripts/normalize.py`: as above. Run with `python3 -I`.
- VectorCraft MCP (`vectorcraft`, headless, scoped to ~/Projects) is available for boolean ops, path cleanup, and export (`vectorcraft-cli commands` lists operations). For simple sprites, writing the SVG directly is usually faster; use VectorCraft for path simplification, Pathfinder unions, and exports (PNG, PSD).
